# EXERCISE 1

## A. Identification

**App name:** `לחיצת חזה במוט — שיפוע חיובי ושטוח`
**Exercise ID / key:** `db-bench-press`
**Best standardized English name:** Barbell Bench Press — one record covering TWO variations: (1) **Incline Barbell Bench Press (~30–40°)** and (2) **Flat Barbell Bench Press**
**Alternative/common names:** Incline Bench Press / Flat Bench Press / BB Bench Press
**Exercise category:** horizontal press (flat) + inclined horizontal press (incline). Bilateral, closed-chain-ish scapular fixation on the bench, open kinetic chain at the hands.
**Equipment:** free straight Olympic barbell + adjustable/olympic bench inside a power/half rack. NOT dumbbells, NOT Smith, NOT machine.

## B. Starting Position
- Body orientation: supine on a bench.
- Torso angle: Video 01 = incline, est. **~30–40°** (low/moderate incline; NOT a 60°+ shoulder-dominant incline). Videos 03/04 = **flat (0°)**. Video 02 = flat by elimination (top-down camera, angle not verifiable).
- Back position/support: full back support; scapulae retracted + depressed into the pad; deliberate lumbar arch, glutes stay on the bench (video 04 shows a clear arch).
- Shoulder: depressed and retracted, held there for the whole set. Video 02 is entirely about this: ❌ shoulders elevated/protracted toward the ears (red delts + pain marks) vs ✅ shoulders pulled down and back (green pecs).
- Elbow: tucked ~45–60° from the torso, NOT 90° flared. At the bottom the forearm is vertical, elbow directly under the wrist (video 03 red-vs-green demo).
- Hip: on the bench, hip in slight extension because of the arch.
- Knee: ~80–90°, feet pulled back under/behind the knees.
- Foot: flat on the floor, roughly shoulder-width or slightly wider, planted for leg drive.

## C. Grip / Contact Position
- Grip type: pronated (overhand), full grip on the bar.
- Grip width: moderately wide — hands roughly **1.4–1.6× biacromial width** (clearly outside the shoulders, not a close/narrow grip).
- Hand position: video 01 and video 04 both dedicate a close-up to it — the bar must sit on the **heel of the palm, in line with the forearm (stacked wrist, ✅ green)**; bar high in the fingers → wrist bent back (❌ red, with an overlay line marking the broken wrist angle).
- Handle type: straight Olympic barbell, standard knurling.

## D. Movement
- Start of the resistance: bar at arm's length above the chest (lockout), taken off j-hooks in the rack.
- End position: bar touching the chest — **upper chest / just below the clavicle on the incline**, **mid-to-lower chest (sternum/nipple line) on flat**.
- Main joint actions: shoulder horizontal adduction + shoulder flexion (more flexion component on the incline), elbow extension, scapular retraction/depression held isometrically (not moving with the bar).
- Movement path: bar descends in a slight arc down-and-slightly-toward-the-feet, then presses up-and-slightly-back over the shoulder joint. On the incline the bar tracks higher, toward the clavicle.
- Elbow path: elbows travel down-and-out at ~45–60°, forearm kept perpendicular to the floor at the bottom; the app's own cue and video 03 both explicitly forbid elbows flaring past the wrists.
- Shoulder/scapula: scapulae pinned retracted + depressed against the pad; shoulders must not roll forward or shrug up at lockout.
- Hip/knee: static; feet planted, mild leg drive into the floor.

## E. Resistance Mechanics
- Resistance source: gravity on a free barbell.
- Direction of resistance: straight down (vertical), constant.
- Cable direction: N/A.
- Machine path: N/A — free bar, so the path is lifter-controlled (no fixed arc, no converging arms).
- Resistance relative to the body: the resistance vector is vertical while the torso is tilted (incline) or horizontal (flat), so the moment arm at the shoulder is largest when the bar is furthest from the shoulder joint — i.e., at the bottom/mid-range. Peak external torque is around chest-touch, and it drops toward lockout.

## F. Range of Motion
- Approximate ROM: full lockout (elbow near-straight) → bar touching the chest.
- Deep stretch position: bar in contact with the chest; shoulder in maximal horizontal abduction/extension for that grip width — this is the stretched position.
- Peak contraction: top/lockout.
- Partial or full ROM: full ROM, bar touches the chest. **But**: the app's own cue says "למעלה לא לנעול מרפקים — לשמור מתח" (don't lock out at the top, keep tension), so the intended execution is full ROM at the bottom, slightly short of lockout at the top.

## G. Execution Details
- Bilateral, simultaneous.
- Closed kinetic chain at the scapula/torso (fixed to the bench), open at the hands.
- Tempo shown: not measurable — the videos are edited technique demos, not a filmed set. NOT determined.
- Special technique: scapular retraction + depression, deliberate arch with glutes down, feet planted, elbows tucked ~45–60°, stacked wrist, bar in the heel of the palm.

## H. Important Variation Details
1. **One app record = two distinct variations.** The Hebrew name and the videos both say incline AND flat. The 4 videos split 1 incline / 3 flat. Any biomechanical analysis must treat these as two exercises.
2. **Barbell, not dumbbells** — despite the ID `db-bench-press`. The ID and the original source folder name ("Dumbbell Bench Press") are stale; the project's own `catalogFix.ts` documents the correction: "ארבעת הסרטונים הם מוט ולא דאמבלים".
3. **Free barbell in a rack, not a Smith machine.** No guide rods on the bar; plates and collars visible; j-hooks and rack uprights ("HOIST STRENGTH" in video 02).
4. **The incline is low-to-moderate (~30–40°), not a steep 45–60° shoulder-dominant press.** The bar tracks to the upper chest, and the athlete's head sits well below the top of the pad.
5. **Elbows tucked, not flared.** Both the app cues and video 03 make this the defining execution detail.
6. **Bar touches the chest** — not a partial/pin press.
7. Grip is moderately wide, NOT a close-grip / triceps-biased bench.

## I. What Is Uncertain?
- **HIGH CONFIDENCE** — free straight barbell; supine on a bench; pronated grip; bar touches the chest; elbows tucked ~45–60°; scapulae retracted+depressed; bilateral; video 01 is an incline and videos 03+04 are flat.
- **HIGH CONFIDENCE** — the app record covers both incline and flat in one entry.
- **MODERATE CONFIDENCE** — incline angle ≈30–40°. Measured from an oblique camera; could plausibly be 30° or 45°.
- **MODERATE CONFIDENCE** — grip width ≈1.4–1.6× shoulder width (estimated visually, no reference scale).
- **MODERATE CONFIDENCE** — video 02 is a flat bench. The camera is directly overhead so the pad angle can't be seen; assigned by elimination from the project's own note (1 incline + 3 flat).
- **LOW CONFIDENCE** — whether the thumb is wrapped around the bar or thumbless. The ✅ close-up doesn't show the thumb clearly.
- **UNKNOWN** — tempo, number of reps/sets in the demo, actual bar load, touch-and-go vs paused.
- **UNKNOWN** — which of the two variations Tavor actually performs on any given session; the app does not record it.
- **CONTRADICTION (data, not video):** the record is stored as `usesPlates: false`, `barWeightKg: null`, `weightMode: 'perSide'`, `seedWeightKg: 22.5`. For a barbell exercise those numbers are internally inconsistent — they are leftovers from when the record was read as a *dumbbell* press (22.5 kg in each hand). The plate calculator is therefore off for this exercise and the bar weight is not accounted for.

# EXERCISE 2

## A. Identification

**App name:** `מקבילים במכונה`
**Exercise ID / key:** `dips`
**Best standardized English name:** **Seated Plate-Loaded Lever Triceps Dip Machine (Machine Dip / Seated Dip Machine)**
**Alternative/common names:** Plate-Loaded Tricep Dips (the video's own on-screen title) · Machine Dip · Seated Dip Machine · Lever Triceps Dip
**Exercise category:** elbow extension–dominant vertical press-down against a lever arm; shoulder extension + elbow extension. **Open kinetic chain** (the handles move, the body does not).
**Equipment:** plate-loaded lever machine (blue frame), saddle seat + foot platform, two fixed neutral-grip dip-bar-style handles at hip level on a single rotating lever/frame. NOT bodyweight parallel bars, NOT an assisted-dip machine, NOT a selectorized stack.

## B. Starting Position
- Body orientation: **seated** on a saddle-type seat pad, feet on a foot platform in front, knees bent ~90°.
- Torso angle: near-upright with a small forward lean, est. **~15–25° forward of vertical**. Explicit coaching in the video: *"make sure that you're sitting upright in order to get the triceps."*
- Back position/support: **no back pad — the torso is free/unsupported**, held upright by the trunk. The seat supports the pelvis only.
- Shoulder: neutral/slightly extended, held down; the video explicitly warns *"don't shrug down to the floor — you'll get your pecs too much"*, i.e. don't let the shoulder girdle sink/travel.
- Elbow: at the start, flexed (roughly 90°+), positioned at the sides of the torso, close to the body, NOT flared out.
- Hip: flexed ~90° in the seat.
- Knee: flexed ~90°.
- Foot: flat on the machine's foot platform, roughly hip-width, providing stability only (no leg drive).

## C. Grip / Contact Position
- Grip type: **neutral (palms facing the body)** — stated verbatim in the video's caption: *"plate loaded tricep dips with a neutral grip."*
- Grip width: fixed by the machine — roughly shoulder-width, one handle at each side of the hips.
- Hand position: at hip level alongside the torso, slightly forward of the hip; forearm hangs approximately vertical.
- Handle type: straight, horizontal, forward-pointing padded dip-bar-style handle stubs on the lever arm (one per side). Not a rope, not a D-handle, not a bar.
- Contact points with the machine: hands on the handles + glutes on the saddle seat + feet on the platform. **The body is supported, so bodyweight is NOT the resistance.**

## D. Movement
- Start of the resistance: handles up, at approximately hip/lower-torso height, elbows flexed.
- End position: handles pressed down and slightly back, elbows fully extended (locked), hands alongside/slightly behind the hips.
- Main joint actions: **elbow extension** (primary) + **shoulder extension** (secondary); scapula held stable, no shrugging.
- Movement path: the handles travel in an arc downward and slightly rearward around the lever's pivot; the torso stays in place.
- Elbow path: elbows stay tucked close to the sides and drive down-and-back behind the body line; the video's final cue is *"pull your elbows [in/back] and you feel those triceps engaging."*
- Shoulder/scapula: kept depressed and quiet — the "don't shrug" cue exists precisely to stop the scapula and chest from taking over.
- Hip/knee path: none — static, support only.

## E. Resistance Mechanics
- Resistance source: gravity acting on loading-pin plates transmitted through a **machine lever arm** (plate-loaded).
- Direction of resistance: opposes the press — resistance pulls the handles **upward/rearward along the lever's arc**, so the athlete pushes down and slightly forward-then-back.
- Cable direction: N/A — lever, not cable.
- Machine path: **fixed arc**, single rotating lever. Both handles appear rigidly linked on one frame → **not independent arms, not converging**.
- Resistance relative to the body: the force line meets the hand from below/behind, alongside the hip. Because a lever arm produces a torque-based curve, the resistance profile is *not* the constant-vertical profile of a bodyweight dip; the exact cam/lever curve of this specific machine cannot be determined from the footage.

## F. Range of Motion
- Approximate ROM: from roughly 90°+ elbow flexion (handles up at hip/torso height) to full elbow lockout.
- Deep stretch position: the top of the movement (handles highest, elbow most flexed, shoulder most flexed). **The video deliberately caps this**: *"when you come up, don't go up so high — it's your front delt."*
- Peak contraction: full elbow extension at the bottom, hands beside/behind the hips.
- Partial or full ROM: **intentionally restricted at BOTH ends.** Not a full-stretch dip. Top capped to keep load off the anterior delt; bottom/depth capped ("don't shrug down to the floor") to keep the pecs out. This is one of the most important facts for any downstream biomechanical analysis.

## G. Execution Details
- Bilateral, simultaneous (single linked lever).
- **Open kinetic chain** — this is the key mechanical difference from a bodyweight parallel-bar dip, which is closed-chain with the body as the moving mass.
- Tempo shown: NOT determined — the clip is a talking demo with slow partial reps, not a counted working set.
- Special technique: neutral grip · torso upright (only a slight forward lean) · elbows tucked to the sides · full lockout at the bottom · no shrug/sink · capped top ROM · body supported by seat + foot platform.

## H. Important Variation Details
1. **This is not a bodyweight dip on parallel bars.** The athlete's body does not travel — I verified this by tracking head height against a fixed machine landmark (the yellow tag on the frame) across the top and bottom of a rep: the vertical distance is essentially unchanged (~1130 px vs ~1115 px). The handles move, the athlete doesn't.
2. **Not a chest/lean-forward dip.** The torso is deliberately kept upright (~15–25°). The video explicitly frames the forward-sinking version as the *mistake* ("you'll get your pecs too much").
3. **Neutral grip, elbows tucked, no flare** — stated in the video.
4. **Deliberately shortened ROM at the top** to keep the anterior delt out — so the exercise is NOT trained through the deep-stretch position that a bodyweight dip trains.
5. **Plate-loaded lever with a fixed arc**, not a selectorized dip machine and not an assisted-dip machine.
6. **Seated with a saddle seat and no back pad** — the trunk is unsupported, so the torso angle is user-maintained rather than machine-enforced.
7. **Name/data conflict inside the app:** the app stores `muscleGroup: 'chest'` for this exercise, while both the English name ("Plate-Loaded Triceps Dips") and the video (titled "Plate Loaded TRICEP Dips", with every cue steering load toward the triceps and *away* from the pecs) are triceps-first. Treat this as triceps-dominant; the stored `chest` primary is a legacy value.

## I. What Is Uncertain?

- **MODERATE CONFIDENCE** — torso lean ~15–25° forward of vertical (measured from hip-to-shoulder pixel geometry on a handheld, orbiting camera).

- **UNKNOWN** — tempo, rep count, and actual plate load in the demo.
